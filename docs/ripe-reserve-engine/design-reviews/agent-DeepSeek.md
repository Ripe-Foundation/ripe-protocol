## 2. Summary and verdicts

**What it is, in buyer terms.** A page that says "pay dollars, get RIPE instantly." You connect a wallet, preview a quote (RIPE out, lock options, exit terms), approve the stablecoin, and call `buyNow` with three guards you filled from the preview (`expectedEpoch`, `minRipeOut`, `deadlineBlock`). The contract takes your exact payment straight to Endaoment Funds, mints new RIPE, and hands it to you — either straight to your wallet (unlocked) or deposited into the core RipeGov vault with a lock you requested. If another buyer consumed the epoch capacity first, your transaction reverts and you re-preview and retry. The next epoch's price is computed by a controller from how much of the previous epoch sold and when in the epoch the money arrived; the first buyer after an epoch boundary pays the new price in the same transaction, so there is no keeper.

**Operator terms.** Governance sets a 17-field config through a timelocked switchboard (Foxtrot): per-epoch payment cap, per-lane lifetime mint budget, rate ceiling and seed, utilization thresholds, up/down step ranges, empty-epoch decay, lock bonus and minimum. A one-shot exact rate override can be installed (timelocked) and is consumed at the next rollover. `canBuyNow` and pause are immediate; a stop wipes the clock and epoch history; the payment token can only be swapped while stopped.

**Assumed top objective.** Raise dollars for Endaoment by selling newly minted RIPE with *bounded dilution and near-zero operator touch*, while keeping RIPE's holder base healthy. I challenge one thing in the ranking, not the top slot: the design spends its sophistication budget on the demand controller (pricing), but in every comparable mechanism the load-bearing parts for the *issuer's* side are caps, time-release, and per-buyer limits — not the pricing rule (Olympus's own DAI-bond post says bonds "require less management" precisely because price is self-regulating, and pairs that with a 5-day vest and a 0.05%-of-supply per-order cap). The controller here is fine; it is just not the part that protects anyone. All recommendations below assume the objective as stated; they would flip if the lane were meant to be RIPE's primary distribution channel (then vesting and per-buyer caps stop being optional).

**One-line verdicts**

- **Support this product concept? Yes.** The shape — capped, keeperless, oracle-free, epoch-priced sale with proceeds to charity — is coherent and the safety layering (cap → budget → ceiling → pause → exact settlement) is the right hierarchy. It is not misbuilt; it is under-told and has three sharp edges.
- **Picked shape:** the current lane, kept, with three bounded changes and two experiments (below). Not the simpler lane, not a different model.
- **Top 3 changes:** (1) give the rate override a freshness bound (expiry after N epochs); (2) make `cumulativeMinted` a one-way ratchet while running and move its setter under the timelock; (3) gate the locked path off at activation so the lane sells only what it can actually deliver, and say so.
- **Vesting: don't add** at launch — but tie it to calibration with a decision gate (see register).
- **Override: keep the one-shot exact pin, add an expiry.** The recorded reason for no expiry is wrong in an important way.
- **Keep the name "Instant Bond"? No.** Rename before activation (cheapest now). "Bond" promises discount + vesting + a claim; this delivers none of the three. "Instant" is the product; "Bond" is baggage from a mechanism family this lane deliberately is not.
- **Epoch-fixed + lazy rollover: keep.** It is the right trade for "no keeper, no oracle, instant."
- **Biggest disagreement with a recorded decision:** owner decisions 21 and 22 plus §6.7 — the override with "no target epoch, no expiry, applies at the first later rollover regardless of how many calendar epochs have elapsed." A timelocked exact pin is already stale by construction; letting it fire at an *unknown, arbitrarily distant* rollover compounds the staleness. The recorded reason ("adds a new signal, authority path, or state machine") overstates what an expiry is: one scalar, one branch at rollover, one event.

**Doc/code conflicts at this commit (named, per instructions):**

True for `setConfig`'s `mintBudget` — but `setCumulativeMinted` (an **immediate**, non-timelocked Foxtrot action) can set the counter to any value ≤ `mintBudget`, **including below the true issued amount**, at any time. **Synthesis — which precedents changed a recommendation (one beat each):**

- **FIP-22 + Olympus DAI bonds** changed my vesting call. The family's strongest lesson is "discount + instant liquidity = flip pipeline," and both Olympus and Frax answered with time-release. I still recommend *against* vesting at launch — but only because the lane does not promise a discount: the rate is controller-set, can price above market, and a vest would tax fair-price buyers and corrupt the utilization signal (utilization would no longer equal demand at that price). The FIP-22 lesson is therefore converted into a **calibration gate**: if approved parameters can sustain below-market pricing for multiple epochs at meaningful size, vesting (or a steeper catch-up step) becomes mandatory. The DAI-bond **0.05% per-order cap** becomes the per-buyer-cap experiment.
- **ATM** sharpened the pricing question. An ATM's entire discipline is "sell at market, cap daily volume, disclose the program." The lane sells at a fixed controller price that can sit below market for several epochs while `maxUpBps` catches up — the leak the docs quantify. This is why change #1 is a *freshness* fix and why the epoch cap must be calibrated like an ADTV cap (the manifest's `maximum_acceptable_late_sellout_catch_up_epochs` is the right gate).
- **UK placings / pre-emption** is the closest ordinary-finance lens on "who bears the dilution." Existing RIPE holders are diluted by every sale with no pre-emption and no discount-to-market bound. I don't recommend adding holder-first-refusal (that's a different product), but it confirms the lane must be *sized* conservatively relative to RIPE float and that the external aggregate ledger is not paperwork.
- **T-bill / Gnosis** confirmed the epoch-fixed price already has the uniform-price property that matters (everyone in an epoch pays the same rate). Batch-at-close would fix timing games but destroys "instant"; rejected for this objective.
- **SDA's restart-above-winning** and **VRGDA's exponential catch-up** both suggest the controller's weakest point is catch-up speed, not step logic — a calibration answer, not a redesign.
- **FXB and inverse bonds** are the required contrasts: FXB shows what a *floor that protects buyers* looks like (the lane has none by design — fine, since the budget/cap protect the treasury side); inverse bonds show the same "bond" machinery running in reverse. Both support renaming.
- Their **§5.1** citations check out (Bond Protocol SDA ✓, GDA/VRGDA ✓, FXB GDA-with-floor ✓, Emissions Manager as governance-issuance reference ✓) and its conclusion — bounded capacity and conservative issuance protect the treasury more than pricing sophistication — matches the evidence. The gap in §5.1 is that it cites FXB (bucket 2, contrast) where **FIP-22 and Olympus DAI bonds** (bucket 1, same product) are the precedents that would have changed the vesting and per-order-cap discussion.

## 4. Three shapes

- **Current lane as-is.** Buyer: quote → approve → `buyNow` → instant RIPE; races revert and retry; lock choice is offered but at activation delivers an unlocked position in practice (RipeGov blends locks; bonus is 0), and a buyer can't bind the vault or minimum lock. Operator: low touch, but carries override hygiene (revalidate after every halt), an external aggregate ledger, depeg monitoring, and a mint counter that must be policed manually because it's writable. Issuance discipline: cap + budget + ceiling + pause are real; the budget counter is soft; there is no market anchor and no per-buyer bound. Complexity: two contracts, 17-field config, dormant lock path + dormant bonus arithmetic, override lifecycle.
- **Simpler lane.** Unlocked-only, no override, controller kept; config timelocked via Foxtrot. Buyer: no lock choice, no surprise semantics. Operator: fewer knobs, but *no price intervention at all* — the one tool to correct a mispriced epoch is gone, which is dangerous exactly when calibration is new and wrong. Issuance discipline: same caps; simpler surface. Complexity: one contract, ~12 fields. The controller is the point of the product; removing the override saves little and costs the only human-in-the-loop price control.
- **Different model: epoch-close uniform-price batch (Gnosis/T-bill style).** Buyers commit during the epoch (limit price ≥ floor); at close, all pay the stop-out rate, pro-rata at the margin, refunds issued. This kills final-block timing games, cap front-running, and gives the controller a true demand curve instead of a utilization binary. It costs: no instant settlement, escrow/refund/claim machinery, a settlement trigger (keeper or first-claimant), and a much bigger contract. It answers a different question ("what rate clears demand") than the one this product asks ("at this rate, how much clears").

**Pick: the current lane, kept — with the three changes below, the rename, and two experiments.** The controller is the product (keeperless, oracle-free, bounded); the batch model is a different product for a different question; the simpler lane throws away the only price intervention. What the lane actually needs is not a different mechanism — it is honesty at the edges: a fresh override, a hard counter, a truthful lock, and a truthful name.

**Top three expanded.**

**1. Override freshness (P1).** *What changes:* `overrideLeadEpochs` (governed, in config) and a stored `installedEpoch`; at rollover, if `elapsed > lead`, discard the target, emit `RateOverrideExpired`, apply the controller rate. *Tradeoff:* operators lose "pin will definitely fire"; they gain "pin can't fire stale." The exact-target semantics stay (a bounded nudge variant is *not* added — keep the knob count). *Precedent and where it breaks:* every timelock in ordinary finance expires (PEG authorities ≤15 months; ATM boards re-authorize), and the SDA's per-auction restart is the same freshness instinct. It breaks if an operator genuinely wants a price held across a long halt — the answer there is reinstall after the halt (already the documented behavior for reopening).

**2. Hard mint counter (P1).** *What changes:* `setCumulativeMinted` reverts unless `_amount >= cumulativeMinted` (while running); downward correction requires `stop()` (like the payment token); Foxtrot queues it through the timelock. *Tradeoff:* deployment of a replacement lane needs the stop→set→start dance (already the pattern for token swaps); the "set to prior issuance" use survives. *Precedent and where it breaks:* ATM program size is board-authorized and disclosed, not operator-resettable intraday; Olympus debt ceilings were governed. It breaks the (unrecorded) convenience of correcting an operator error instantly — a stop is the escape hatch.

**3. Truthful locked path (P1).** *What changes:* an activation gate making the lane unlocked-only until isolated RipeGov lock lots exist (matches what `maxLockBonus=0` already implies operationally); quote shows `actualLock=0` and no lock marketing; when the path re-enables, add `_minActualLock` + `_expectedCoreVaultId` bindings (restoring the R23 selection R24 removed). *Tradeoff:* the dormant Teller path stays tested but off — a small honesty tax instead of a misleading feature. *Precedent and where it breaks:* Bond Protocol tokenizes fixed-term bonds so the buyer holds a contractual claim; FXB is a trustless 1:1 promise. RipeGov's share-weighted blend can't make that promise (the 15-RIPE/100k-position example in the threat model is the concrete failure). It breaks nothing except the option to market "buy locked RIPE" at activation.

| `actualLock`, `ripeGovVaultId`, `canExitEarly`, `exitFee`, `isExitFrozen` | Vault terms are live (SwitchboardAlpha); core vault can rotate; "impossible range" silently unlocks | **Nothing** — no min-lock or vault binding (fixed by change #3 at activation) | Re-preview; can't assert lock |
| `available` (market-readiness) | Wallet balance/allowance, Endaoment liveness, RIPE pause/blacklist NOT checked (documented) | Not bindable | `buyNow` can still revert "payment failed"/"mint failed"; UI must preflight |
| `minPaymentAmount`, `budgetRemaining` | Config writes (prospective); budget cuts are live | Budget revert "mint budget" | Show live budget in UI |
| `deadlineBlock` (input) | — | Binds expiry; no min window | UI: short default |

**Override lifecycle (with the proposed freshness bound):** *queue* (timelocked install; same-epoch purchases leave it pending; preview shows it) → *activate* (first later successful rollover stores the exact target and consumes it; counterfactual `controllerRate` emitted) → *expire* (no rollover within `overrideLeadEpochs` → discarded, controller rate applies, `RateOverrideExpired`) → *cancel* (timelocked, any time) → *invalidate* (`setConfig`/`start`/`stop` clear it). Pause/`canBuyNow` do not touch it.

## 7. Open questions (only ones that would move the picked shape)

1. **Does RIPE have meaningful secondary-market liquidity, and a price?** If yes, "discount to market" is measurable and the leak analysis is real; if no, the lane *is* the price and half the pricing critique evaporates. This is the biggest unknown and it changes calibration, not shape.
2. **Who is the expected buyer — retail donors, or arbitrageurs/whales?** If the former, the per-buyer cap experiment and the charity framing matter more; if the latter, the lane is fine as-is and the cap is the ADTV analog.
3. **Program size relative to RIPE float and RipeGov-locked supply?** Dilution tolerance is what sizes `mintBudget`/`paymentCapPerEpoch`; the pre-emption analogy says existing locked holders are the constituency being diluted and should see this sized conservatively.
4. **Is the lane a marginal fundraising tool or a primary distribution channel?** The brief's own framing says "bounded cap-clearing demand controller, not a fair-value oracle" — under that framing my picks stand. If it becomes the main way new RIPE reaches people, vesting and per-buyer caps move from gates/experiments to P1.
5. **Is there a RipeGov roadmap for isolated lock lots?** If yes, the locked path is a roadmap feature and change #3 is a gate; if no, the locked path is dead code and should be *removed* (true simplification), not gated.
