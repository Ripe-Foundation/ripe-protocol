## 2. Summary

**Assumed top objective.** The docs and manifest read as: (1) grow Endaoment reserves with a dollar asset, (2) with as little operator touch as possible, (3) distribution and governance lock as side benefits, (4) price discovery explicitly not a goal. I would flip the top two and put **issuance discipline first**: for a thin-market token, the binding question is not "how much can we raise" but "how far below the open market will we sell before the controller notices." Everything in the precedent record (Frax's Olympus-Pro program, ATM equity, Olympus RBS/EM) says volume-only controllers are slower than markets, and the leak goes to arbitrage bots, not to the community. That re-ranking drives the three top changes below. I am not treating governance lock as an objective: with the lock bonus at zero and RipeGov blending every deposit into one account-level unlock, the lane cannot deliver it, and trying to (a lock bonus or a forced floor) revives the exposure the 15 Aug decision correctly shut.

**What the lane is, in plain terms.** A standing "buy RIPE from the treasury at today's posted price" window. Each epoch has one price; the first buyer after a boundary sets the new price from how much of last epoch's cap sold and buys at it. The buyer pays a dollar stablecoin straight to Endaoment Funds and gets freshly minted RIPE in the wallet, or — optionally — dropped into the core RipeGov vault with a lock. Hard bounds are a per-epoch payment cap, a lifetime mint budget, and an all-in RIPE-per-dollar ceiling. Governance can pause, disable, change config through a timelock, and install one exact next-epoch rate.

The mechanism is sound and well-bounded, and its engineering is careful. The product around it is not yet: the thing it protects against (a runaway controller) is not the thing that will hurt it (a slow controller under arbitrage); the lock path can surprise a buyer; four governance levers are sharper than the docs admit; and the name promises a financial instrument the buyer does not receive.

- **Support the concept?:** Yes — a bounded, keeper-free, posted-rate primary sale is a durable pattern (I bonds, ATM programs, Olympus v2 → Bond Protocol). Not as a "bond."

- **Picked shape:** **A simpler lane**: same contract skeleton and clock, controller collapsed to one up-step and one down-step, ceiling treated as the real price control, lock made opt-in and honest, four levers de-sharpened.

- **Top 3 changes:** (1) Calibrate and run the controller as a one-step ratchet with the ceiling as the binding control (`minUp=maxUp`, `minDown=maxDown=decay`, stranding invariant, ceiling re-ratcheted by governance). (2) "Unlocked means unlocked": one assert in `buyNow`, UI defaults to unlocked, lock via Teller. (3) De-sharpen the immediate levers: monotonic `setCumulativeMinted`, sticky `canBuyNow`, timelocked `start`/token swap, immediate cancel of an installed override.

- **Vesting:** **Don't add.** Every vesting rail surveyed either became a tradable claim that re-prices the discount on a secondary market or a per-user footgun its own issuer removed.

- **Override:** **Keep blunt** (one-shot exact, timelocked) and fix two sharp edges: installed-override cancel becomes immediate; install is allowed before the first epoch so a restart is price-continuous. Bounded multi-epoch nudge (Olympus v1 `setAdjustment` shape) is the experiment, not the launch.

- **Keep the name "Instant Bond"?:** **No.** Rename before deployment (event names, `INSTANT_BOND_LANE_ID` and ABI freeze it). Recommended: **RIPE Direct Sale** (`DirectSaleLane`); acceptable: "Buy Now lane", which the README already uses.

- **Epoch-fixed + lazy rollover:** **Keep.** It is the discount-window / I-bond / ECB-2003 pattern, it needs no keeper, and it has a fifth of Bond Protocol's parameter surface. Continuous (Olympus v1 / SDA) and batch (Gnosis) both cost more than they return here.

- **Biggest disagreement with a recorded decision:** Revision 24 dropped the **minimum-actual-lock binding** that the 15 Aug owner decision (item 8) selected, with no recorded reason, and the tests now pin the consequence: a buyer who previewed an *unlocked* quote can be settled into a RipeGov lock with an 80% exit fee. Close second: §5.1 lists SDA/FXB/GDA as precedents and omits the closest one — Olympus v1/Pro volume-only pricing — whose measured Frax outcome is the strongest evidence about this design.

### Which precedents changed a recommendation

- **Frax FXS via Olympus Pro** — the only measured outcome of an oracle-free, volume-only governance-token sale: ~18% realised discount against a <10% target, 40–55% off during rallies despite an instant multisig lever. It moved me from "current lane as-is" to "simpler lane with the ceiling as the binding control," and it killed the per-tx cap idea (orders were simply split).

- **Olympus v1 `setAdjustment`** — bounded, rate-limited BCV steps applied lazily by buyers' transactions. That is the shape of a bounded override; it is why the override row says "experiment: nudge," not "replace with exact."

- **Olympus v1 merge-and-reset, v2 cliff notes, Bond Protocol ERC-1155/20 claims** — every vesting rail either became a tradable discounted claim or a per-user footgun. That settles vesting: don't add.

- **Bond Protocol's ten coupled parameters** (three contract versions, audit findings on interactions) — the argument for collapsing the Lane's controller to three knobs rather than adding any.

- **ATM programs and TAF** — disciplined sellers idle most of the time and the floor is the price when demand fades. That reframed "stranding" from a liveness bug into the normal state, and justified sizing decay conservatively and adding a fill-monitoring runbook.

- **I bonds and the ECB 1999–2000 tender** — epoch-fixed posted rates are durable, but a predictable below-market posted price plus a cap produces a rush at the open. That is why the timing weighting should be collapsed (it rewards the rush and is gameable by a monopolist) and why the first-block race should be accepted rather than engineered around.

- **Olympus Emissions Manager / Convertible Deposits** — the one live 2026 seller is oracle-gated with a 110–120%-of-spot floor and retunes daily. It defines the "different model" in §4 and shows what the Lane would need to adopt if RIPE's market ever gets deep enough for a reference price.

- **Maker SBE → BEAM** — ten retunes in fifteen months, then a bounded operator module. Operators will touch the lane more than the docs expect; design the levers for that.

- **ESPP / discount DRIP / PIPE** — locks without a priced bonus attract nobody; per-address caps are sybil-defeated. That rules out "make the lock do distribution work" and supports launching with the lock de-emphasised.

- **Basis / ESD** — the naming row.

- **Gnosis batch auction** — rejected as the replacement model (needs settlement and escrow; can clear below every bid when thin), which is what makes "keep epoch-fixed + lazy rollover" a positive choice rather than a default.

## 4. Three shapes

### A. Current lane as-is

- **Buyer experience:** Preview, then buy at one posted price per epoch with a payout floor and deadline; capacity races revert and retry. Lock is a checkbox that pays nothing, can be forced by a config change landing between preview and buy, and blends into any existing RipeGov position (a 1-day request on top of a 3-year position stays ~3 years).

- **Issuance discipline:** Bounded by cap, budget and ceiling; but the controller's working step under adversarial timing is `minUpBps`, the dead band is a free faucet for a single buyer, and after one strong epoch the capped decay can leave the lane priced above the market with no buyer to trigger a rollover. The fixture (not calibration) needs 36 epochs to catch a 2× move under last-block selling.

- **Operator load:** Low day-to-day, high in incidents: override latency is timelock plus rest-of-epoch; restart re-prices at seed; four immediate levers (`setCumulativeMinted`, `start`/`stop`, token swap, stale config re-enabling `canBuyNow`) are sharper than the threat model states.

- **Complexity:** 17 config fields, 14 events, two contracts; the timing amplifier and the four-knob down branch add calibration surface that the adversarial case never uses.

### B. A simpler lane — picked

- **Buyer experience:** Same flow, two honest promises added: "unlocked means unlocked" (one assert), and the UI defaults to unlocked while the bonus is zero, routing "I want to lock" through Teller where the blended unlock can be shown. Quote adds epoch-end and pending-override context; reverts get reason codes or a client simulation path.

- **Issuance discipline:** Controller collapsed by config to one up-step and one down-step equal to decay, with the stranding invariant enforced; the ceiling is set as the price the treasury will actually accept and re-ratcheted by governance against an off-chain reference — the Frax/ATM lesson. Dead-band monitoring and a fill runbook.

- **Operator load:** Lower in incidents: installed-override cancel immediate; override installable before the first epoch so restarts are price-continuous; sticky `canBuyNow`; monotonic `cumulativeMinted`; cadence and token changes timelocked.

- **Complexity:** Contract deltas are small (one assert, one monotonic check, one Foxtrot action type or validation bound, one branch in `_getEpochSnapshot`); everything else is config, UI and docs. Fewer live knobs than today.

### C. Reference-priced sale (Olympus EM/CD shape)

- **Buyer experience:** Buy at "oracle price plus a premium," with price stepping up per tick filled; instant RIPE. Feels like an ATM program: no discount, just availability.

- **Issuance discipline:** Best of the three when the oracle is honest: never sells below a governed fraction of spot. RIPE's only on-chain source today is a spot read of one Aerodrome RIPE/WETH pool (`AeroRipePrices.vy`) — manipulable in the direction that makes the lane cheaper, so the guard can be attacked exactly when it matters.

- **Operator load:** Olympus retuned its version 30–1000× in nine months and it switched itself off in a drawdown. An oracle adds a monitoring and pause surface the manifest already struggles to staff.

- **Complexity:** New signal, new authority path, new failure mode — the three things the deferred list rightly refuses for v2. Right model later, when a TWAP-grade reference exists; wrong model now.

**Why B.** A beats nothing it needs to beat; C solves the real risk with a tool the protocol does not yet have. B keeps every bound and the keeper-free clock, removes the calibration surface that only helps a monopolist, and turns the ceiling into the control the evidence says actually protects treasuries. It also respects the owner's standing minimise-contract-changes directive: the three contract edits are each a few lines and none touches the settlement path.

### Expanded: the three highest-priority changes

#### 1. Run the controller as a one-step ratchet, with the ceiling as the control **[P1]**

**What changes.** Config only: `minUpBps = maxUpBps`, `minDownBps = maxDownBps = decayBps`. Qualifier and simulator gain the invariant `(1/(1−decay))^maxDecayEpochs ≥ 1 + upStep`. Calibration treats `upStep` as the working step (the fixture's 2% is far too small; the "late" catch-up rows show 14/36/117 epochs for +30%/2×/10×). `maxEffectiveRate` is set as a real minimum acceptable price against an off-chain reference and re-ratcheted by timelocked config on a cadence; an alert fires when the projected rate pins to the ceiling.

**Tradeoff.** You give up the information in early-versus-late demand. The record says that information is real only when two or more bots are racing, and in that regime it rewards the race; in the single-buyer regime it is fully selectable and always yields the minimum. You also give up severity-scaled down-steps, which only matter if genuine retail partial fills are common — which nothing suggests at launch. You keep a simpler operator mental model and one fewer way to mis-set the anti-ratchet.

**Precedent and where it breaks.** ATM floor prices and Bond Protocol's `minPrice` are the controls that survived; Frax shows what happens when the floor is absent and the controller is the only bound. It breaks if the ceiling is set lazily — a ceiling parked far below market is no control at all — so the re-ratchet cadence is part of the change, not an afterthought. It also breaks if governance sets the ceiling *above* market in a downturn: then the lane stalls (Frax FXB's zero-fill rounds), which is the intended, monitored state rather than a failure.

#### 2. "Unlocked means unlocked," and lock through Teller **[P1]**

**What changes.** One assert in `buyNow` after `_calculatePayout`: a zero lock request that would settle locked reverts. The UI defaults to unlocked while `maxLockBonus = 0`, and a "lock my RIPE" path uses the existing `Teller.depositIntoGovVault` / `adjustLock` after showing the buyer their blended post-deposit unlock (computable from `RipeGov.userGovData` + `getWeightedLockOnTokenDeposit`). Docs §5/§16 add the longer-direction example: 10,000 RIPE with ~47.0M blocks remaining plus 100 RIPE requested at the 43,200-block floor blends to ~46.5M blocks — "lock for a day" becomes "lock for three years."

**Tradeoff.** A lane-level `minLockDuration` policy still works, but the client must pass it explicitly; a zero request against a nonzero floor becomes a revert instead of a silent upgrade. That is the point. You lose the one-transaction convenience of lock-on-purchase for buyers with existing positions; they gain an honest number.

**Precedent and where it breaks.** ve(3,3) moved from Curve's per-account blend to per-position NFTs because the blend surprised depositors; Stargate's locked sale tranche became a governance dispute; PIPE holders sell the day a restriction lifts. It breaks only if the owner wants the lane to *force* locks as policy — in which case the forced lock should be a first-class argument the buyer signs, not a live config field that can flip between preview and buy. This row overrides Revision 24's "no buyer-bound lock fields" and spec L999-1001; the recorded reason there is ABI minimalism under a size ceiling that Revision 24 itself dropped. It is consistent with the 15 Aug owner decision item 8.

#### 3. De-sharpen the immediate levers **[P2]**

**What changes.** `setCumulativeMinted` accepts only values ≥ the current counter (its legitimate use — carrying a retired lane's issuance forward — is upward). `setConfig` keeps the live `canBuyNow` so a stale queued config cannot re-open a disabled lane. `start` (which installs `epochLength`) and `setPaymentToken` become timelocked Foxtrot actions, or at least `_isValidEpochLength` gains a governed minimum. Cancelling an *installed* override becomes immediate. `stop`, pause and disable stay immediate.

**Tradeoff.** Cadence and token changes slow to timelock speed; since both already require a stop and a config that is itself timelocked when decimals differ, the practical cost is small. A fourth Foxtrot action type adds a few hundred bytes — both contracts are far under EIP-170 and the local ceilings were dropped.

**Precedent and where it breaks.** Every ordinary-finance regime in the table applies term changes prospectively and puts issuance-increasing changes behind a slower process than issuance-stopping ones (ESPP "no adverse effect on an offering already made," Treasury's regular-and-predictable doctrine, Maker's post-2020 GSM delay). It breaks only in a genuine emergency that requires *raising* issuance fast — which is not an emergency.

### Override lifecycle (picked shape)

- **Queue:** Governance calls `setInstantBondRateOverride(target)` on Foxtrot; validated against the live ceiling; timelocked; a queued action can be dropped immediately with `cancelPendingAction` or left to expire. Visible on-chain — buyers can see an announced cut or raise; an announced raise empties the current epoch, an announced cut cannot outrun a leak. Pause first when either matters.

- **Activate:** Execute installs the exact target in the lane (allowed before the first epoch in the picked shape). Preview projects it without consuming it; same-epoch buys leave it pending; the first later successful rollover stores it exactly, emits the counterfactual controller rate, and consumes it. A reverted purchase restores it.

- **Expire:** Queue expiry only. An installed target has no expiry — keep that (exactness) but make "revalidate or cancel before reopening after a long halt" a hard manifest checklist item, because a probe purchase also consumes it and sets up a down-step.

- **Cancel:** Installed-override cancel becomes *immediate* (governance-only, de-escalation to the controller path). Any full config write, `start`, or `stop` still invalidates it.

- **Pause:** Override survives pause/disable. Paused epochs decay at the next rollover unless an override is installed; the refutation pass confirmed a short pause after a sold-out epoch makes RIPE dearer or unchanged, not cheaper — only long gaps or a low-fill stored epoch cut price. Reopen runbook: check projected rate, install or cancel override, then enable.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Who is the intended buyer?** If the lane is meant for retail through the Ripe UI, shape B is right and the dead-band/timing concerns are second-order. If it is meant to clear size to arbitrageurs, the reference-priced shape C should be scheduled rather than left as an experiment, because the Frax outcome is the expected outcome.

2. **Does BondRoom keep running alongside?** Two primary venues for the same asset at different prices create a third arbitrage (between lanes) and split the demand signal the controller reads. A single live venue at a time — or BondRoom reserved for bad-debt episodes — changes the calibration.

3. **Was the Revision-24 removal of the lock/vault bindings ratified?** If yes, record it and I withdraw the "biggest disagreement" line but keep the assert (it is a smaller, different binding). If no, the code overrides an owner decision and should be brought back into line before calibration.
